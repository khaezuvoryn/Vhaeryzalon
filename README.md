# Vhaeryzalon

> The loom wove a shadow. Its owner had not yet entered the light.

## The House of Silent Thread

Vhaeryzalon stands at the end of a road paved with fragments of blue pottery. None of the fragments bears a pattern, but rain briefly gathers them into the outline of a vessel too large to have belonged inside any house.

The house contains a loom, a stone basin, and seven stools arranged around a window. The window faces a courtyard that cannot be reached through the door. Those who leave the house find a hillside instead, covered in grass whose roots shine faintly after sunset.

The loom works without a weaver. Its wooden arms move during the hours when the window reflects nothing. By morning, a length of cloth lies across the floor, showing the shadow of something absent from the room.

## A Visitor Carrying No Cloth

The first visitor described in the household records arrived barefoot, carrying a silver spool. His cloak was dry despite the rain. Beneath its hem, the witnesses saw scales where they expected skin, and a long shadow whose wings folded against the pottery road.

He placed the spool inside the basin. The silver thread uncoiled, rising toward the loom without touching the floor. The wooden arms stopped. In the window, the courtyard became crowded with stools, each occupied by a figure whose face was turned away.

The visitor asked which seat had been left for him. The witnesses counted seven stools inside the house and could not count those outside. He accepted their silence as an answer and departed through the window.

His footprints remained upon the glass until winter. Nobody could determine which side held them.

## The Cloth beneath the Eighth Stool

A carpenter noticed that the seven stools cast eight shadows. He examined their legs and found nothing unusual, except that the eighth shadow remained round when the others became narrow.

He attempted to trace it with chalk. The circle shifted beneath his hand, becoming an image of the stone basin. Inside the image, a small creature slept around a silver spool, its wings tucked beneath its own tail.

The carpenter covered the tracing with cloth from the loom. The cloth acquired the shape of a stool. He folded it carefully, but every fold added another wooden leg to the shape beneath it.

By dawn, the covered object stood beside the window. The carpenter refused to uncover it. Its shadow had the unmistakable shape of a person holding chalk.

## The Question in the Basin

The basin contains no water. Nevertheless, visitors can hear a drop falling when they lean close to its rim. The sound is followed by a voice asking a question the listener nearly understands.

One listener heard a request for a name. Another heard a request for a room. A third heard the voice of her mother, asking why the courtyard had been left outside the house.

The household wrote down every account. Their pages remained blank while they were read indoors. Outside, the words appeared as threads sewn through the paper, reaching toward the shining grass.

A keeper followed those threads downhill. He returned holding a blue pottery fragment upon which a small window had been painted. Through it, he could see himself still walking away from the house.

## The Spool's Unfinished Language

**Vhaer — yz — alon — kheth.**

These sounds appear in the weaving whenever the silver spool turns inside the basin. No hand turns it. Its motion begins when someone seated beside the window forgets why they came.

The first sound is woven in blue. The next two have no visible color, but can be felt as raised lines beneath a closed palm. The last appears only in the cloth's shadow, separated from the others by a narrow image of the courtyard.

An interpreter proposed reading the sequence backward. Before she could finish, all seven stools faced the basin. Their shadows remained facing the window.

She left her interpretation unwritten. A new thread emerged from the loom, silver on one side and dark on the other. It was warm wherever her fingers had touched the unfinished cloth.

## The Weaver's Missing Reply

> Who owns the shadow upon the floor?
>
> Whoever has not chosen a place in the window.
>
> What does the loom remember?
>
> The hand that left before the thread arrived.
>
> Why does the basin hold no water?
>
> Because the falling drop is still inside the question.

These exchanges were recorded by the carpenter while sleeping beside the covered stool. He remembered no speaker. His dream contained only a courtyard filled with empty seats and a silver spool turning beneath a pale wing.

Upon waking, he found chalk upon his palms. The covered stool had moved closer to the basin, although its shadow remained where he had slept.

## Where the Road Loses Its Pattern

The house still receives visitors. They leave their pottery fragments beside the door and sit upon whichever stool appears unoccupied in the window's reflection.

The loom continues weaving. The basin continues asking its nearly familiar question. On rainy nights, the blue road briefly resembles a vessel carrying a house through a sea of luminous grass.

Vhaeryzalon waits at the end of that resemblance. Beneath the covered stool, a shadow holds a piece of chalk and draws the hand that has not yet returned.
